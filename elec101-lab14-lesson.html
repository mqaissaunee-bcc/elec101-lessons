Section 14.1

Instrumentation amplifiers

Bridges, differential and common-mode signals, and the three-op-amp design.

Many sensors, such as strain gauges and thermistors, change resistance very slightly. Wired into a bridge, that change becomes a tiny voltage difference of a few millivolts, sitting on top of several volts that both sides share. An instrumentation amplifier amplifies the difference and ignores the shared part.

Differential and common-mode signals

  • The differential signal is IN+ − IN-: what you want to measure.
  • The common-mode signal is what both inputs have in common: here about 3 V from the bridge, plus any noise picked up equally on both wires. A good instrumentation amplifier rejects it.

The three-op-amp design (Figure 14-1)

  1. Two input buffers (U1A, U1B) draw almost no current from the bridge, so they don't disturb it. Together with RA1, RB and RA2 they amplify the difference by 1 + 2RA ÷ RB = 21 but pass the common-mode voltage through unamplified.
  2. A difference amplifier (U1C) subtracts its inputs, removing the common-mode part, and multiplies by RF ÷ RIN = 100.
  3. The overall gain is 21 × 100 = 2100. A 1 mV imbalance becomes 2.1 V at OUT.

Section 14.2

Mirroring parts

Why Lab 14 mirrors instead of rotating, and how to do it in Q Circuits.

Both Lab 14 drawings require parts to be mirrored, not rotated. Rotating turns the whole part; mirroring flips it like a reflection, so the pins swap sides but the part still faces the same way.

Mirroring in Q Circuits

  • Select the part and press ⇆ (Mirror horizontally: left and right swap) or ⇅ (Mirror vertically: top and bottom swap). They're on the toolbar next to Rotate. PSpice calls these Mirror Horizontally and Mirror Vertically.
  • Pins, pin labels and the symbol all follow. Wires don't move, so after mirroring check that every wire still meets its pin.
  • 14A: U1A is mirrored vertically so its + input is on top. U1B and U1C are not mirrored. The lab checks all three.
  • 14B: J2 and Q3 are mirrored horizontally, so their gate and base face right. The lab checks both.

Lab 14A: drawing Figure 14-1

The instrumentation amplifier, its mirrored op amp and its no-dot crossing.

Open 14A · Instrumentation amplifier and draw Figure 14-1.

Choose a part of the circuit to highlight it, or select a part.

Select a part.

  1. Type LAB 14A in the circuit name box.
  2. Place the split supply: VS1 and VS2 (DC 18) with VCC and VEE power symbols and the junction grounded.
  3. Draw the bridge: VS (DC 6) across two dividers, R1 over R3 and R2 over R4, all 10k except R2 = {RVAL}. Place a Parameter part: RVAL = 10k.
  4. Place three LM324s. Select U1A and press ⇅ so its + input is on top; U1B and U1C stay as placed.
  5. The R2–R4 junction is IN-, to U1A's + input. The R1–R3 junction is IN+, to U1B's + input. The IN+ wire crosses the R2–R4 line without joining it: no dot.
  6. RA1 (10k), RB (1k) and RA2 (10k) run in a column from U1A's output to U1B's output. U1A's − input joins the RA1–RB junction, and U1B's − input joins the RB–RA2 junction.
  7. RIN1 (10k) runs from U1A's output to U1C's − input, and RF1 (1MEG) from there to OUT. RIN2 (10k) runs from U1B's output to U1C's + input, and RF2 (1MEG) from there to ground.
  8. Power every op amp from VCC and VEE (remember U1A's supply pins are swapped), name U1C's output OUT, and press Check my work.

Inside an op amp

The stages of Figure 14-2 and the new parts it uses.

Every op amp you've used since Lab 5 is a complete multi-stage amplifier on one chip. Figure 14-2 is a simplified op amp built from discrete parts, so you can see what's inside.

N-channel JFET

Toolbar: NJF, Model J2N3819

A junction field-effect transistor. The gate voltage controls the drain–source current, and the gate draws almost no current.

More about the JFET
  • Pins: gate (left, arrow pointing in), drain (top), source (bottom).
  • Why here: an op amp's inputs should load the circuit as little as possible; a JFET gate is nearly an open circuit.
  • The Q Circuits J2N3819 model is PSpice's, with a few parameters removed that the simulator can't accept.

D1N914 and D1N750

Toolbar: D, choose the Model

D1N914 is a small-signal diode (a 0.7 V drop when forward biased). D1N750 is the 4.7 V zener from Lab 5.

More about the diodes
  • D1 and D2 are forward biased: they cancel the transistors' base–emitter drops.
  • D3 is reverse biased in breakdown: it holds a steady 4.7 V reference. Place it with its anode toward vee.

Named power symbols

Toolbar: Power symbol

The handout places VCC/CAPSYM symbols named vcc, vee, vin+, vin- and vo. In Q Circuits use the Power symbol and type each name.

More about the names
  • Symbols with the same name are connected, like net aliases.
  • Q Circuits keeps vin+ and vin- as separate nodes, even though SPICE node names can't contain + or -.
  • Rotate a symbol so its bar points away from the wire it labels.

Section 14.5

Lab 14B: drawing Figure 14-2

A discrete op amp, stage by stage.

Open 14B · Inside an operational amplifier and draw Figure 14-2. It's the largest drawing in the course: take it in stages.

Choose a stage to highlight it, or select a part.

Select a part.

  1. Type LAB 14B in the circuit name box.
  2. Place every part first, lining up the columns as Figure 14-2 does: R1–R12, J1 and J2 (NJF, J2N3819), Q1–Q6 (NPN, Q2N2222), D1 and D2 (D1N914) and D3 (D1N750).
  3. Select J2 and Q3 and press ⇆, so J2's gate and Q3's base face right.
  4. Draw a vcc rail across the top and a vee rail across the bottom, and label them with power symbols named vcc and vee. Add power symbols named vin+, vin- and vo for the inputs and output.
  5. Wire it as Figure 14-2 shows. Check every crossing against the figure: a dot means the wires join; no dot means they only cross.
  6. Front end: R1, D1 and D3 bias Q1, whose collector feeds both JFET sources. vin+ drives J1's gate and vin- J2's gate. R2 and R3 load the drains.
  7. Second stage: J2's drain drives Q2's base, and J1's drain crosses over to Q3's base. R4 and R5 are the collector loads; the emitters share R7.
  8. Output: Q3's collector drives Q4. R8, D2 and R9 bias Q5 under R10 and R11. Q4's emitter, through R10, drives Q6's base, and Q6's emitter is vo, loaded by R12. Press Check my work.

“No ground” is expected. Like the handout's figure, this circuit has no ground: its supplies and inputs come from outside the sheet. Parts and connections will say No ground; the lab is marked on the drawing, so that's fine here.

Glossary

Dotted-underlined words in the lesson show their definition when you point at, tap or tab to them. The full course glossary (opens in a new tab) is searchable.

Instrumentation amplifier
Amplifies a small difference and rejects the common part.
Bridge
Two voltage dividers side by side; their difference shows a small change.
Common mode
The voltage both inputs share.
Mirror
Flip a part left–right or top–bottom without rotating it.
JFET
A transistor controlled by gate voltage, with almost no gate current.
Differential pair
Two matched transistors sharing a current; their difference steers it.
Current source
A circuit that supplies a steady current.
Emitter follower
A transistor stage that copies a voltage with low output resistance.
