A

AC (alternating current)
Current or voltage that keeps reversing direction, usually as a smooth sine wave.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: ac, alternating current
AC magnitude
The size of the test signal a source supplies during an AC sweep. PSpice's VAC source sets it with 1Vac.
Introduced in Lab 5, Lab 6
Also: vac
AC sweep
An analysis that feeds the circuit a sine wave at many frequencies and plots how the output changes with frequency.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: ac sweep analysis
Active-low
An input that does its job when it is 0, not 1. Its name usually has a bar or BAR, as in SBAR or CLR̅.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: active low
Amplifier
A circuit that makes a signal larger.
Introduced in Lab 5, Lab 12
Also: amplifiers
Amplitude
How big a wave is: the distance from its centre line to its peak.
Introduced in Lab 5
Analysis panel
The Q Circuits panel where you choose what the simulator calculates (Operating point, DC sweep, Transient or AC sweep) and its settings.
Introduced in Lab 5
AND gate
A logic gate whose output is 1 only when all of its inputs are 1.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: and
Anode
The terminal of a diode that current enters when the diode conducts. It's the triangle side of the symbol.
Introduced in Lab 5
Axis ranges
The Q Circuits settings under the plot that fix the X and Y limits instead of fitting them automatically. PSpice calls this a User Defined data range.
Introduced in Lab 8, Lab 9
Also: axis range, user defined range

B

Band-pass filter
A filter that passes a middle range of frequencies and weakens both lower and higher ones. Lab 5A's circuit is one.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: band-pass
Bandwidth
The range of frequencies between a circuit's two cutoff (−3 dB) points.
Introduced in Lab 5
Base
The control lead of a transistor. A small base current controls a much larger collector current.
Introduced in Lab 4
Bias
The DC voltages and currents that put a transistor in its working range, so it can amplify.
Introduced in Lab 12
Also: biasing
Bias point
PSpice's name for the Operating point analysis: the steady DC voltages and currents in a circuit.
Introduced in Lab 5
Bit
A single binary digit: 0 or 1. It can stand for any two-way choice, such as off/on or false/true.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: bits
Block diagram
A drawing that shows a system as labelled boxes, such as amplifier or filter, rather than individual parts.
Introduced in Lab 4
Bode plot
A graph of gain in decibels against frequency on a logarithmic axis. An AC sweep with a probe in dB mode produces one.
Introduced in Lab 8
Also: bode plots
Boolean expression
A formula for a logic function, such as Q = A + B for OR or Q = A · B for AND.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: boolean
Branch
One of the separate paths current can take between two nodes in a parallel circuit.
Introduced in Lab 6
Also: branches
Breakdown voltage
The reverse voltage at which a diode starts conducting backward. A Zener diode is designed to do this safely; the D1N750's is 4.7 V.
Introduced in Lab 5
Bridge
Two voltage dividers side by side. A small change in one resistor shows up as a small voltage difference between the two junctions.
Introduced in Lab 14
Also: wheatstone bridge
Bus
A group of signals drawn as one thick line. Signals join it by name, through bus entries.
Introduced in Lab 13
Also: buses, busses
Bus entry
The short diagonal line that ties a named wire to a bus.
Introduced in Lab 13
Also: bus entries
Bypass capacitor
A capacitor that acts as a short circuit for AC across a resistor, so the resistor affects DC but not the signal.
Introduced in Lab 12

C

Capacitor
A part made of two conductors separated by an insulator. It blocks steady DC and passes changing signals, more easily at higher frequencies.
Introduced in Lab 5
Cascade
Amplifier stages connected one after another. Their gains multiply, so their gains in dB add.
Introduced in Lab 12
Cathode
The terminal of a diode that current leaves when the diode conducts. It's the bar side of the symbol, and a painted band on a real part.
Introduced in Lab 5
Circuit name box
The box at the top of the Q Circuits page where you type the lab name. It does the job of a title block.
Introduced in Lab 4
Clear
A flip-flop input that forces Q to 0 regardless of the clock. On the 7473 it is active-low (CLR̅).
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: clr
Clipping
When an amplifier's output tries to go beyond its supply voltage and is flattened at the limit.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: clips
Clock
A digital signal that switches back and forth at a steady rate to tell sequential circuits when to act.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: clock signal
Closed circuit
A complete, unbroken loop that current can flow around.
Introduced in Lab 4
Collector
The transistor lead where the main current enters an NPN transistor.
Introduced in Lab 4
Combinational logic
Logic whose output depends only on its present inputs, with no memory. Every Lab 10 circuit is combinational.
Introduced in Lab 10, Lab 11
Common mode
The voltage both inputs of a differential amplifier share. A good differential amplifier ignores it.
Introduced in Lab 14
Also: common-mode
Common-emitter amplifier
A transistor amplifier with the input at the base and the output at the collector.
Introduced in Lab 12
Also: common emitter
Complement
The opposite logic level: the complement of 1 is 0. A NOT gate produces it. Written with a bar, as Q̅ (QBAR).
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: complementation, negation
Corner frequency
The frequency where a filter's output has fallen to 70.7% of its passband value (−3 dB). For an RC filter it's 1 ÷ (2πRC).
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: cutoff frequency, cutoff frequencies, corner frequencies, −3 dB point, -3 db point
Counter
A sequential circuit that steps through a sequence of binary numbers, one per clock pulse.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: binary counter
Coupling
How much of a transformer's magnetic field links both windings, from 0 to 1. 1 would be a perfect transformer.
Introduced in Lab 8
Also: coupling coefficient
Coupling capacitor
A capacitor that passes an AC signal from one stage to the next while blocking DC.
Introduced in Lab 12
Also: coupling capacitors
Critical frequency
Another name for the corner (−3 dB) frequency: where the output has fallen 3 dB below its flat level.
Introduced in Lab 8
Crossover
A place where two wires cross on a schematic without connecting. There is no dot, and in Q Circuits neither wire ends there.
Introduced in Lab 6
Also: crossovers
Current
The flow of electric charge, measured in amperes (A). 1 mA is one thousandth of an ampere.
Introduced in Lab 4
Also: current (i)
Current source
A circuit that supplies a steady current regardless of the voltage across it.
Introduced in Lab 14
Also: current sink

D

DC (direct current)
Current or voltage that stays steady and flows in one direction.
Introduced in Lab 4
Also: dc, direct current
DC sweep
An analysis that steps a source's DC value through a range and plots the results against it.
Introduced in Lab 7
Decade
A factor of ten in frequency, such as 10 Hz to 100 Hz. On a log axis every decade is the same width.
Introduced in Lab 5, Lab 8
Also: decades
Decibel (dB)
A logarithmic unit for gain: 20 × log₁₀ of the voltage ratio. ×10 is +20 dB, ×1 is 0 dB, ×0.707 is −3 dB.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: decibel, decibels, db
Decoder
A circuit that turns a binary number into one active output line. The 74154 is a 4-to-16 decoder.
Introduced in Lab 13
Demultiplexer
A circuit that routes one input line to one of many outputs, chosen by select lines.
Introduced in Lab 13
Also: demux
Differential amplifier
An amplifier built from two matched transistors that responds to the difference between two inputs. Lab 6C draws one.
Introduced in Lab 6
Differential pair
Two matched transistors sharing a current. The difference between their inputs steers the current from one to the other.
Introduced in Lab 14
Differential probe
A probe that measures the voltage between two nodes instead of from a node to ground, such as V(A,B). Placed with Diff probe (X).
Introduced in Lab 7
Also: diff probe
DigClock
The Q Circuits and PSpice digital clock source, set by OFFTIME, ONTIME, DELAY, STARTVAL and OPPVAL.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: digital clock
Digital stimulus
A digital source defined by a list of times and logic levels. PSpice calls it STIM1.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: stim1, stimulus
Diode
A part that lets current flow easily in one direction only, from anode to cathode, once about 0.7 V is across it.
Introduced in Lab 5
Duty cycle
The fraction of each period a pulse spends high: pulse width ÷ period, often given as a percentage.
Introduced in Lab 9

E

Emitter
The transistor lead where current leaves an NPN transistor. It's the lead with the arrow.
Introduced in Lab 4
Emitter follower
A transistor stage whose output (the emitter) follows its input with low output resistance.
Introduced in Lab 14

F

Fall time
How long a pulse takes to drop from its high level back to its low level (TF).
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: tf
Falling edge
The moment a signal changes from 1 to 0. The 7473 changes its output on the clock's falling edge.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: negative edge, falling-edge
Family of curves
Several traces on one plot, one for each value of a parameter, so you can compare them.
Introduced in Lab 7
Feedback
Sending part of an amplifier's output back to its input. In an op amp circuit, feedback sets the gain.
Introduced in Lab 5
Filter
A circuit that passes some frequencies and weakens others.
Introduced in Lab 5
Flip-flop
A one-bit memory circuit that changes state only at a clock edge.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: flip-flops
Floating node
A node with no DC path to ground. The simulator can't work out its voltage.
Introduced in Lab 6
Also: floating
Forbidden state
An input combination a latch should avoid. In a NAND latch, SBAR and RBAR both 0 drive Q and QBAR both to 1, and releasing both at once leaves the result unpredictable.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: invalid state
Frequency
How many cycles a wave completes each second, measured in hertz (Hz). 1 kHz is 1,000 cycles per second.
Introduced in Lab 5
Frequency response
A graph of how a circuit's gain changes with frequency. An AC sweep produces one.
Introduced in Lab 5

G

Gain
How much a circuit multiplies a signal: output divided by input. Often given in decibels.
Introduced in Lab 5
Gain-bandwidth product
For an op amp, gain × bandwidth is roughly constant: about 1 MHz for the LM324.
Introduced in Lab 12
Also: gbw
Ground
The reference point defined as 0 V. Every voltage is measured from it. Also called node 0.
Introduced in Lab 4
Also: ground (node 0), node 0

H

High-pass filter
A filter that passes high frequencies and weakens low ones.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: high-pass

I

Inductor
A coil of wire that stores energy in a magnetic field. Its opposition to AC rises with frequency. Measured in henries (H).
Introduced in Lab 8
Also: inductors, inductance
Input node
The node where the signal enters a circuit. Looking from ground through the source, it's the node on the other side.
Introduced in Lab 6
Instrumentation amplifier
An amplifier that boosts a small difference between two inputs and rejects what they have in common.
Introduced in Lab 14
Integrated circuit
A chip containing many components. A 7400-series chip holds several gates, labelled A, B, C, D, as in U1A.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: ic, chip
Inverter
A NOT gate: its output is the opposite of its input. The 7404 holds six of them.
Introduced in Lab 10

J

JFET
Junction field-effect transistor: the gate voltage controls the current, and the gate draws almost no current.
Introduced in Lab 14
Also: n-channel jfet
JK flip-flop
A flip-flop with inputs J and K: 0 0 holds, 1 0 sets Q to 1, 0 1 resets it to 0, and 1 1 toggles it.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: jk
Junction
A point where three or more connections meet, marked with a dot.
Introduced in Lab 4
Also: junction dot

L

Latch
A memory circuit that responds to its inputs as soon as they change, with no clock. Two cross-coupled NAND gates make an RS latch.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: rs latch, sr latch
Linear circuit
A circuit whose output is directly proportional to its input. Its DC sweep plot is a straight line.
Introduced in Lab 7
Also: linear
Load line
A straight line showing every combination of voltage and current the rest of the circuit allows. Where it crosses a part's curve is the operating point.
Introduced in Lab 5
Loading
When connecting more parts to a node draws current from it and lowers its voltage.
Introduced in Lab 7
Also: load
Logarithmic scale
An axis where each factor of ten takes the same space, so 10 to 100 is as wide as 1,000 to 10,000.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: logarithmic, log scale
Logic gate
A circuit that produces a 0 or 1 output from one or more 0/1 inputs according to a fixed rule.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: gate, gates, logic gates
Logic lanes
The way Q Circuits plots digital signals: one 0/1 strip per probe, top to bottom in the order the probes were placed.
Introduced in Lab 10, Lab 11
Also: logic lane, lanes
Logic level
The 0 or 1 a digital signal represents. In Q Circuits, 0 V is 0 and 5 V is 1, with the threshold at 2.5 V.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: logic state, logic levels
Low-pass filter
A filter that passes low frequencies and weakens high ones.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: low-pass

M

Master-slave flip-flop
A flip-flop made of two latches: the master follows the inputs while the clock is high, and the slave copies it when the clock falls.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: master-slave
Measurements table
The table under the Q Circuits plot that lists each trace's maximum, minimum and other values over the visible range.
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: measurements
Mid-band gain
An amplifier's flat gain between its lower and upper −3 dB frequencies.
Introduced in Lab 12
Minor gridlines
The fainter lines between the main gridlines of a plot. On a log axis they mark 2, 3, 4 … 9 times each decade.
Introduced in Lab 5
Mirror
Flip a part left–right or top–bottom, as a reflection, without rotating it.
Introduced in Lab 14
Also: mirrored, mirroring
Modulus
How many different states a counter steps through before repeating. A 4-bit counter is MOD 16.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: mod
Multiplexer
A circuit that sends one of many inputs to a single output, chosen by select lines. Taking turns, it sends many signals over one line.
Introduced in Lab 13
Also: mux

N

NAND gate
NOT AND: the output is 0 only when all inputs are 1.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: nand
Net alias
A name attached to a wire, such as IN or OUT. It labels the node, and wires with the same name are connected.
Introduced in Lab 5, Lab 6
Also: net aliases
Netlist
The text version of a schematic: a list of every part and the nodes it connects to. It's what the simulator actually reads.
Introduced in Lab 4
Node
All the points joined together by wire. Every point on a node is at the same voltage.
Introduced in Lab 4
Non-inverting amplifier
An op amp circuit where the signal enters the + input. Its gain is 1 + RF ÷ RIN, and the output is in step with the input.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: non-inverting amplifiers
Nonperiodic waveform
A signal that never repeats in a regular pattern, such as random noise.
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: nonperiodic
NOR gate
NOT OR: the output is 1 only when all inputs are 0.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: nor
NOT gate
A gate with one input whose output is the opposite level. Also called an inverter.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: not
NPN transistor
A transistor made of N-type, P-type and N-type layers. Current flows in at the collector and out at the emitter.
Introduced in Lab 4

O

Ohm's law
The link between voltage, current and resistance: I = V ÷ R, or V = I × R.
Introduced in Lab 4
Op amp (operational amplifier)
A very high-gain amplifier with + and − inputs. Resistors around it set the actual gain.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: op amp, operational amplifier, op amps
Open circuit
A break in the path. No current can flow through it.
Introduced in Lab 4
Open end
A wire end or pin connected to nothing. Q Circuits marks it with an open circle.
Introduced in Lab 4
Operating point
An analysis that calculates the steady DC voltage at every node. PSpice calls it Bias Point.
Introduced in Lab 4, Lab 5
OR gate
A logic gate whose output is 1 when any input is 1.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: or
Orthogonal wiring
Wires drawn only as horizontal and vertical segments, the professional standard.
Introduced in Lab 4
Also: orthogonal
Output node
The node where you measure a circuit's result. Looking from ground through the output part, it's the node on the other side.
Introduced in Lab 6

P

Parallel
Parts connected across the same two nodes. They share the same voltage.
Introduced in Lab 4
Parameter
A named value, such as RVAL, defined by a Parameter part and used in a part value inside curly braces: {RVAL}.
Introduced in Lab 7
Also: parameters, {rval}
Parametric sweep
Running the same analysis several times, once for each value of a parameter, and plotting all the results together.
Introduced in Lab 7
Period
The time for one complete cycle of a repeating waveform. Period = 1 ÷ frequency.
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: per
Periodic waveform
A signal that repeats the same shape at regular intervals.
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: periodic
Phase shift
How far one wave is shifted in time relative to another, measured in degrees; a full cycle is 360°.
Introduced in Lab 5
Plot
The Q Circuits graph of probed signals. Volts and amps are drawn in separate panes.
Introduced in Lab 5
PN junction
The boundary where P-type and N-type semiconductor meet. It's what makes a diode conduct in one direction.
Introduced in Lab 5
Port
A named connection drawn as an arrow (PSpice's PORTLEFT and PORTRIGHT).
Introduced in Lab 13
Also: ports
Power symbol
A named supply connection such as VCC or VEE. Every symbol with the same name is the same node.
Introduced in Lab 5, Lab 6
Primary
The transformer winding connected to the source.
Introduced in Lab 8
Also: primary winding
Probe
A marker that tells the simulator what to graph: a wire for its voltage, or a part's body for its current. Placed with the Probe tool (B).
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: probe tool, probes
Propagation delay
The short time between a gate's input changing and its output responding, typically nanoseconds.
Introduced in Lab 11
Pulse source
A source that switches between two voltages with set delay, rise, width, fall and period. PSpice calls it VPULSE.
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: pulse, vpulse
Pulse width
How long a pulse stays at its high level (PW).
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: pw

Q

Q Circuits
The browser-based schematic and simulation tool used for the ELEC 101 labs.
Introduced in Lab 4

R

Ramp
A waveform that rises steadily and then drops quickly, also called a sawtooth.
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: sawtooth, ramp wave
Reactance
A capacitor's (or inductor's) opposition to AC, in ohms. For a capacitor, Xᴄ = 1 ÷ (2πfC), so it falls as frequency rises.
Introduced in Lab 5
Rectifier
A circuit that turns AC into DC. A single diode makes a half-wave rectifier, which passes only one half of each cycle.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: half-wave rectifier
Reference designator
A part's name on a schematic, such as R1, C2 or Q1.
Introduced in Lab 4
Resistance
How strongly a part opposes current, measured in ohms (Ω).
Introduced in Lab 4
Also: resistance (r)
Resistor
A part that opposes current by a set amount, measured in ohms.
Introduced in Lab 4
Rise time
How long a pulse takes to climb from its low level to its high level (TR).
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: tr
Roll-off
The part of a frequency response where the gain falls steadily. A simple RC filter rolls off at 20 dB per decade.
Introduced in Lab 8
Also: rolloff

S

Schematic
A drawing of a circuit that uses standard symbols to show every part, its value, and how the parts are connected.
Introduced in Lab 4
Secondary
The transformer winding connected to the load.
Introduced in Lab 8
Also: secondary winding
Select lines
The inputs of a multiplexer or demultiplexer that choose which line is connected.
Introduced in Lab 13
Also: select line, select inputs
Sequential logic
Logic whose output depends on past inputs as well as present ones, because it has memory. Latches, flip-flops and counters are sequential.
Introduced in Lab 11
Series
Parts connected end to end with nothing branching off between them. They carry the same current.
Introduced in Lab 4
Series-parallel
A circuit that combines series and parallel connections.
Introduced in Lab 4
Set and reset
Making a memory circuit's output 1 (set) or 0 (reset).
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: set, reset
Short circuit
A path of (almost) zero resistance, such as a wire across a part, so current bypasses the part.
Introduced in Lab 4
Also: short
Simulation
Using a computer to solve a circuit's equations and predict what a real circuit would do.
Introduced in Lab 5
Sine source
A voltage source whose value is SIN(offset amplitude frequency), used for transient runs. PSpice calls it VSIN.
Introduced in Lab 5, Lab 6
Also: vsin
Slope
How steep a line is: how much the output changes for each 1 unit change of the input.
Introduced in Lab 7
SPICE
The family of circuit simulators that Q Circuits and PSpice are based on. It also names the value notation they share, such as 1k and .15u.
Introduced in Lab 4
Square wave
A pulse waveform that switches sharply between two levels.
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: square waves
Step
The spacing between values in a sweep. From −12 to 12 in steps of 0.1 gives 241 points. PSpice calls it Increment.
Introduced in Lab 7
Also: increment
Step-up transformer
A transformer whose output voltage is larger than its input. Its secondary inductance is larger than its primary.
Introduced in Lab 8
Also: step-down transformer, step-up
Synchronous counter
A counter whose flip-flops all share one clock, so every bit changes at the same moment.
Introduced in Lab 11

T

Time constant
For an RC circuit, τ = R × C. In one time constant a capacitor charges 63% of the way; after about five it is essentially done.
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: tau, τ
Timing diagram
A plot of digital signals against time, one above another, used to read how a circuit behaves.
Introduced in Lab 10, Lab 11
Also: timing diagrams
Title block
The labelled box on a drawing that identifies it and its author. In Q Circuits the circuit name box does this job.
Introduced in Lab 4
Toggle
Switch to the opposite state. A JK flip-flop with J = K = 1 toggles at every clock edge.
Introduced in Lab 11
Also: toggles
Transformer
Two coupled coils that transfer AC from one circuit to another, stepping the voltage up or down and isolating the two sides.
Introduced in Lab 8
Also: transformers
Transient
An analysis that steps through time and plots signals the way an oscilloscope shows them.
Introduced in Lab 5
Also: transient analysis
Triangle wave
A waveform that rises and falls at equal, steady rates.
Introduced in Lab 9
Also: triangle
Truth table
A table listing a logic circuit's output for every combination of inputs.
Introduced in Lab 10
Also: truth tables
TTL
Transistor-transistor logic, the family the 7400-series chips belong to. It uses about 0 V for 0 and 5 V for 1.
Introduced in Lab 10

U

Unknown state (X)
A logic value the simulator can't determine, drawn as X. Flip-flops start in X unless they are initialized.
Introduced in Lab 10, Lab 11
Also: x, unknown

V

Value
The number that sets a part's size, such as 1k for a resistor or DC 12 for a source.
Introduced in Lab 4
Voltage
The electrical push that makes charge move, measured in volts (V). Always measured between two points.
Introduced in Lab 4
Also: voltage (v)
Voltage divider
Two or more parts in series that share a voltage in proportion to their resistance (or reactance).
Introduced in Lab 4, Lab 5

W

Wire
A connection with no resistance. Its length and shape don't matter, only what it connects.
Introduced in Lab 4
Also: wires

X

XOR gate
Exclusive OR: the output is 1 when the inputs differ.
Introduced in Lab 10, Lab 11
Also: xor, exclusive or

Z

Zener diode
A diode designed to conduct backward at a precise breakdown voltage. Used as a voltage reference.
Introduced in Lab 5
